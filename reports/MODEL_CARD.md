# Model card: 30-day hospital readmission risk

Format follows Mitchell et al., *Model Cards for Model Reporting* (2019). Numbers are generated by the training pipeline for this exact model version.

## Model details

- **Version:** `20261011.211408-294a166` (created 2026-10-11T21:14:08+00:00)
- **Type:** logistic with grouped cross-validated sigmoid calibration; selected over the LACE index and alternative models by cross-validated average_precision
- **Inputs:** 28 point-in-time features derived from HL7 FHIR R4 Patient, Encounter, Condition, Observation, and MedicationRequest resources
- **Output:** probability of an acute readmission within 30 days of discharge, a risk tier, and the main contributing factors
- **License:** MIT

## Intended use

- **Primary use:** prioritizing post-discharge outreach by care managers and transitional care teams in a value-based care program.
- **Users:** care managers and clinicians, as decision support with human review.
- **Out of scope:** diagnosis or treatment decisions; denying or limiting care, coverage, or benefits; pediatric patients; any use on real patients before local validation, governance approval, and prospective monitoring.

## Training and evaluation data

- **Source:** synthetic FHIR R4 (readmit generator). No real patient data was used.
- **Cohort:** 6,570 index hospitalizations (adults, alive at discharge, full 30-day follow-up); readmission rate 14.6%.
- **Split:** temporal; trained on discharges before 2023-12-22 (with a 30-day embargo), tested on later discharges.

## Performance (held-out, most recent discharges)

- AUROC 0.803 (0.758–0.839); AUPRC 0.446 (0.325–0.543); Brier 0.0988 (0.0842–0.1136)
- Calibration slope 1.05, intercept -0.13
- At 10% capacity: sensitivity 34.6%, PPV 52.5%

## Fairness

Race and ethnicity are excluded from model inputs and used only for auditing. Largest observed gaps: sex AUROC gap 0.010; race AUROC gap 0.202; ethnicity AUROC gap 0.004; age_band AUROC gap 0.141. See section 7 of the evaluation report for per-group metrics.

## Ethical considerations

- **Label choice:** readmission is a utilization outcome, and utilization reflects access to care as well as need. Obermeyer et al. (Science, 2019) showed how a utilization-type label (cost) can encode racial bias in a population-health algorithm; subgroup calibration should be monitored for this reason.
- **Human oversight:** outputs prioritize outreach; they never replace clinical judgment.
- **Privacy:** the copilot receives only minimum-necessary, de-identified context.

## Caveats and recommendations

- Trained on synthetic data; performance on real populations is unknown.
- The CMS planned-readmission algorithm is not applied.
- Monitor feature drift and realized performance; recalibrate or retrain when the quality gate's criteria are no longer met.
